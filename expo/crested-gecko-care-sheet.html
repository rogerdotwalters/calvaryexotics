Crested Gecko Care Guide

Everything you need to set up and care for a crested gecko, from the breeder's bench in Tyler, TX: enclosure, temperature, humidity, feeding, handling, and what to watch for.

Scientific nameCorrelophus ciliatus
OriginNew Caledonia; nocturnal, arboreal
Adult sizeAbout 8 in with tail; 35–55 g
Lifespan15–20 years
Temperature70–80°F (never above 80°F)
HumidityMist every other day, let it dry between
DietPrepared crested gecko diet, every other day
LightingDay/night cycle; low UVB optional

Enclosure

  • Tall and vertical. Height matters more than floor space.
  • Juveniles: 12×12×18 in. Adults: 18×18×36 in or larger.
  • Plastic or glass with some ventilation. Never full-screen (all mesh); it cannot hold humidity.
  • Climbing and hiding space at several heights: cork bark, branches, vines, and plenty of foliage.
  • Feeding ledge or dish up off the floor.

Substrate & Bioactive

  • Paper towel for hatchlings and new arrivals; coconut fiber or a bioactive mix for an established gecko.
  • Bioactive uses live plants plus a cleanup crew of springtails and small isopods.
  • Skip large isopods. A gecko can swallow bedding while hunting them, risking impaction.
  • Avoid sand, gravel, and fine loose substrate for hatchlings. Never use cedar or pine.

Temperature & Lighting

  • Room temperature is usually right: 70–80°F. Overheating is the bigger risk.
  • Most Texas homes need no heat lamp. Keep away from sunny windows, vents, and warm electronics.
  • 10–12 hours of light, then full dark. A timer helps.
  • UVB is optional. If used, low level (2–5%) with plenty of shade and foliage.

Humidity & Water

  • Wet-then-dry cycle. Mist until leaves and walls have droplets, then let it dry out.
  • Plastic with small vents: mist every other day.
  • Glass with open screen top: mist twice every other day.
  • Use dechlorinated or filtered water. Offer a clean shallow water dish too.
  • Soaked or musty enclosure? Mist less.

Feeding

  • Staple: a complete prepared crested gecko diet mixed with water to a thin, ketchup-like paste.
  • All ages: fresh food every other day. Remove leftovers after about 24 hours.
  • Insects are optional but help growth: weekly for babies and juveniles, about monthly for adults. Dust with calcium with D3.
  • Fruit is not needed. Too much sugar is not good for them.
  • Weigh on a gram scale now and then to catch weight loss early.

Handling

  • Give a new gecko 1–2 weeks to settle. Make sure it is eating and drinking first.
  • Short sessions of 5–10 minutes in the evening. Keep low, over a bed or sofa, since they jump.
  • Let it walk hand to hand. Never grab or pull the tail. A dropped tail does not grow back.
  • Skip handling during shedding or if it seems stressed.

Signs to Watch For

  • Stuck shed on toes or tail tip: raise humidity; see a vet if it does not clear.
  • Sunken, wrinkled eyes or skin: dehydration.
  • Weight loss or refusing food for more than a couple of weeks.
  • Bent jaw or limbs, kinked spine or tail, tremors: possible metabolic bone disease.
  • Bubbles at the nose, wheezing, open-mouth breathing: possible respiratory infection.
Note General information, not veterinary advice. If unsure, contact an exotic vet. Quarantine any new animal away from your others.

Shopping Checklist

  • Tall enclosure (18×18×36 in; smaller for juveniles)
  • Substrate, cork bark, branches, foliage
  • Crested gecko diet and feeding dish
  • Thermometer, hygrometer, mister, light timer
  • Gram scale; optional low-output UVB
